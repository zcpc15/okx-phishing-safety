## What fake OKX sites actually look like

Phishing kits that mimic OKX are sold and reused constantly, so the specific URLs change weekly. One documented example flagged by security researchers is `okxclaim.web.app`, which imitates the official site and lures visitors with a fake "rewards" claim. Other variants use look-alike domains with subtle character swaps (`ok×.com` with a multiplication sign, `okxl.com`, `0kx.com`).

The hooks are usually one of these:

- A **fake airdrop or rebate** page that asks you to "connect your wallet" to claim tokens — the connection drains the wallet.
- A **fake customer-service agent** who messages you in Telegram or in-app chat, claims there's a problem with your account, and walks you into entering credentials on a look-alike site.
- A **fake listing-event** page that demands a "listing fee" or "security deposit" before your token can be listed. OKX does not collect listing fees via social-media DMs.

The defense is mostly mechanical: stick to `okx.com`, verify any channel through the official tool, and treat any DM — even one with an "official" looking profile — as fake until proven otherwise.
